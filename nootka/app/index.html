    How to read this

    • One timeline is one possible future of one design. It shows what can happen, not what will.
    • No yearly mortality rate is put into the model. Each year, each tree faces the causes of that year (unwatered dry summer, heat wave, second dry summer in a row, wind storm, shade, end of lifespan) and one draw decides whether it dies. The yearly mortality reported here is counted from the result. Causes that are not modelled (pests, disease, vandalism, planting failure) would add to it.
    • Glade management in this version removes the deciduous tree that shades the shrub patches most, every five years, whatever its size. Real glade management is not done this way. The rule is left as it is on purpose: it shows that this kind of decision needs the designer's judgement.
    • The bird results test habitat conditions (nest trees, cover, food, flowering months). They do not count birds.
    • A tree is marked stressed when it has grown clearly less than it could (dry summers, shade), was broken by snow in the last three years, or is past its expected life. These thresholds are judgement and still to be reviewed.
    • Trees that die are replanted the next year while the site is under active care, so a tree standing at year 50 may be younger than 50.
    • In the section, where the crown starts on the trunk is a fixed share for now. Buildings are drawn for context (3 m per storey) and do not yet shade the trees in the calculation.
    • All drawings use 0.5 m blocks. Ground and buildings in the block model use 1 m columns.
